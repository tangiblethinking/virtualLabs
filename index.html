Odysseyware Virtual Science Labs

Translating physical classrooms into intuitive digital experiences. Bridging the gap between tactile science experiments and scalable LMS architecture.

Project Context & Scope

Odysseyware required a modern, scalable solution to bring 20 high-fidelity virtual science labs (covering subjects from Osmosis and Circuitry to Frog Dissection) into their existing ecosystem.

Team & Leadership

I led end-to-end design, directing a team of junior designers while establishing tight cross-functional alignment with Subject Matter Experts (SMEs), Instructional Designers, Curriculum Writers, QA engineers, and the VP of Product.

My Scope

My responsibilities spanned from initial problem/solution alignment and Information Architecture (IA) to detailed wireframes, UI specifications, and delivering fully functional prototypes ready for the engineering handoff.

Challenges & Constraints

The core challenge was recreating real-world, hands-on labs (e.g., frog dissections, mechanical physics) in a way that felt authentic to a classroom setting. The digital interface needed to be highly intuitive and engaging for students in varying grades, without abstracting away the learning objectives of the physical experiment.

Leveraging behavioral UX principles like Hick's Law, I designed the interface strictly from a student’s curious perspective, paired with instructor oversight. It was critical that students weren't overwhelmed by competing UI elements—toolsets, simulation mechanics, anatomy overlays, and educational text had to be progressively disclosed.

The solution required responsive and reactive layouts to render flawlessly across desktop and mobile devices. Additionally, strict constraints imposed by the Odysseyware LMS framework required complex user states: authentication protocols, audit trails, and specific checks and balances to validate accurate interactions for both students and teachers.

Design Process & Solutions

Structuring the Student Journey

The ecosystem required a seamless transition logic. I architected user flows where a student would intuitively navigate from a standard lesson module directly into a virtual lab environment, perform the experiential task, and transition flawlessly into built-in assessments. The architecture supported repeating virtual labs asynchronously as needed for study or remediation.

Interaction Paradigms

Creating realism without physical touch required specific interaction models. I designed UX logic to incorporate precision clicking/drag-and-drop mechanics for biological dissections, real-time reactive data tracking UI for physics labs, and persistent, immediate feedback loops for progress tracking so students always understood their context within the experiment.

Validating with the Classroom

I led cross-functional workshops and conducted direct usability testing with actual target students, while consulting real classroom science teachers as subject matter experts. The major UX pivots and interface refinements we executed prior to launch were directly informed by observing how students naturally interpreted (or misinterpreted) digital tools compared to physical ones.

Outcomes & Reflections

The Reception

Upon release, the UX was received with overwhelming enthusiasm by educators and students alike. The success of the initial 20 modules validated the architectural approach, driving executive buy-in for further expansion and the commissioning of new virtual science content.

Addressing the Cracks

Post-launch observation is where true UX maturity is tested. Public industry reviews (like the EdTech Roundup article) highlighted real-world friction points, such as software bugs from complex environments and workflow disconnects where quizzes tested materials out of sync with the lab steps.

I utilized these edge cases proactively. I addressed them through stakeholder interviews, rigorous follow-up retrospectives, and an open-feedback loop policy. This operationalized the critique, driving continuous UI/UX improvements and showcasing how senior leadership must evolve a product beyond its initial V1 release.
